Ablign

Takes

The take everything else lines up with — usually your lead vocal. It is never moved.

The takes that get moved onto the guide. Tick as many as you like — a backing pair, left and right, goes through in one pass.

Alignment

How much of the measured correction to apply. At 100% the take sits exactly on the guide. Lower it to leave the take deliberately loose, so two voices still sound like two people rather than one thick one. 0% changes nothing.

How far Ablign may look when hunting for a word. Too small and it cannot reach a take that drags badly; too large and it risks locking onto a similar-sounding word further away.

Steadies the correction. Inside a held vowel every instant looks alike, so the raw match jitters — this removes the jitter, not the correction. Less is not tighter: at 5 ms the result lands ten times further from the guide than at 60 ms.

How hard the gap between two words may be stretched or squeezed. A correction needing more than this comes out only partly applied. Lower it if a take warbles where it had to stretch a long way.

Breath, room tone and headphone bleed sit below this level and get ignored. Raise it for a noisy take, lower it for a very quiet one.

Result

Where the aligned takes land. Replace puts the aligned version on the track, and Live keeps the take it displaced on a take lane underneath, so you can flip between the two. A new track plays straight away and deactivates the take it stands in for over the selected range — a longer clip is split first, so whatever lies outside carries on playing, and reactivating the clip in Live brings the original back. A take lane keeps the original playing and parks the aligned version below, silent until you swap the lane in.
